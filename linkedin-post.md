Build Club ran a Women in AI Accelerator this spring. Four weeks to build something and ship it. I spent mine building for court staff.

Not the lawyers, not the judges. The people who keep a courthouse running. And the AI advice meant for them basically doesn't exist. Every piece of guidance out there seems written for somebody who isn't them.

Quick context. I work in a courthouse. I'm a Judicial Services Manager at SBSC, and the data my colleagues handle every day is about as sensitive as it gets. Case parties. Witnesses. Victims. Jurors. Sealed records. Every other think piece this year is telling court staff to use AI, and not one of them mentions any of that.

So I built the thing I kept wishing existed. It's called Themis Lex.

You tell it your role, describe your work, and say how sensitive your data is. It hands you back two columns: where AI can help you, and where it should never come near your work. You download it as a PDF and walk it straight to your supervisor. No account, no login, nothing you type gets stored. For a tool that asks court staff about their work, "we keep nothing" wasn't a feature I added. It was the point.

Here's the part that still gets me. I'm self-taught. Two years ago I worked in jury services and couldn't have told you what an IAM role was. I built this one solo, on my own time.

Now the fine print, because it matters. Themis Lex is a personal project. It's not affiliated with, endorsed by, or in use at any court, including the one I work for. My day job is why I understood the problem. It's not a sponsor of the solution.

It's also a proof of concept, not a finished product. Three roles, one court's public job descriptions, plenty of rough edges. I'd much rather tell you that now than have you find out later and feel oversold.

It's live. Go poke at it, break it, tell me what's wrong with it. That's the whole reason it has a URL.
